Who is the "default user" when we design technology?

We often design products and services for "everyone," but the people behind the design may still have a very specific user in mind. This can have real consequences for women — from car safety testing and protective equipment to everyday technology.

In this hands-on Design Thinking workshop, we will start with a few real-world examples and a short introduction to the gender lens. Then, working in small teams, participants will choose one of three concrete challenges:

• A female founder pitching to investors: how might we reduce gender bias in the investment process without creating a separate process for women?

• An AI-based recruitment tool: how might we identify and reduce gender bias in candidate selection without simply adding a "women's quota"?

• A product or technology designed for everyone: can you identify where its design may unintentionally favor one gender, and redesign it?

Teams will choose a problem, understand the people affected, and design a product, service or digital solution. They will create a simple prototype, test another team's idea through a gender lens, and improve their design.

The point is not to design something "for women". It is about learning to notice the assumptions that can exclude people and using that insight when building technology and services for everyone.

No previous experience with gender studies or Design Thinking is required. The workshop is about learning by doing, experimenting, and building something together.
