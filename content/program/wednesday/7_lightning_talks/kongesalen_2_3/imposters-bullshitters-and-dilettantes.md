Imposter syndrome is self-reflection gone awry and turned into destructive self-doubt. It is obviously bad and something that we would like to combat. But there is a faulty inference lurking in the shadows: that since imposter syndrome is harmful, there can be no imposters. Everyone is genuine, no-one is bluffing, we're all just flawed humans doing our best. It would be nice if it were true, but lived experience suggests otherwise, and recently things seem to have taken a turn for the worse. The way certain newfangled tools are being put to use has revealed that our commitment to veracity is weaker than we have made it out to be. In a time when the value of human creativity, expertise, judgment and skill is under attack, it is more important than ever to talk openly about the less pleasant tendencies and traits of human nature. Hence in this talk, we'll look at the other side of the coin: the problem of actual imposters, bullshitters and dilettantes.
