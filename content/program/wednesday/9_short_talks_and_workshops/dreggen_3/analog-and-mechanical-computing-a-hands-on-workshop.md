We live in what looks like the peak of digital computing. The AI accelerators from NVIDIA and others that power today's models can only be manufactured by a handful of the most advanced machines and technicians on the planet, working in tightly synchronised global supply chains. Against that backdrop, analog and mechanical computing has become an almost forgotten art and craft.

Yet it is far from obsolete. For education and motivation it is unbeaten: you can touch the computing parts, watch them move, and intuitively grasp why a result comes out the way it does. And there is a hard engineering argument too — analog computation can be orders of magnitude more energy efficient than its digital counterpart, which is why there are serious efforts to fuse analog elements back into AI chips.

In this workshop we build computation you can hold in your hands. We start by combining ready-made cams that act as analog deterministic finite automata, wiring them together into working machines. Then we design and build our own cams from scratch, turning an idea into a physical shape.

To make the behavior visible, we drive robots with our constructions — at minimum drawing lines on paper, and, if our cams are good enough, performing a few additional antics.

Participants leave having experienced computing through touch, sight and sound. For developers whose daily work is entirely abstract and digital, this physical intuition is a genuinely different way of understanding what a machine actually does — and a memorable one to pass on to others.

No prior mechanical or electronics experience required. Materials provided.
