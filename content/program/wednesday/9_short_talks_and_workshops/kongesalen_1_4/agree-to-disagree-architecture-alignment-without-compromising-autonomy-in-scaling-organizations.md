The architect's tough choice: Autonomy or alignment, pick one! 

In most organizations product teams argue for more autonomy, while management demands more alignment. It seems like a stalled situation. As an architect both sides turn to you to mediate this tension. 

In scaling organizations the challenge remains on maintaining some form of alignment, acknowledging that majority of architecture decisions are made by product teams. We can achieve this balance by defining as few as possible of topics the teams need to agree upon, while allowing for autonomy on other matters.

This talk builds on a story from labor union/insurance domain when building a set of architecture guidelines with the sole purpose of aligning product teams on essential principles for architectural decisions. 
We will follow the story from two perspectives:
- The actual architecture decisions being made by the product team, regarding boundaries, dependencies and data ownership 
- The architect's role in defining the guidelines and principles up-front and continuously coaching and observing the decision making in the organization.
