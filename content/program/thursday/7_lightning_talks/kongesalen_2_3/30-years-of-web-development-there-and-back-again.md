In 1994, the server rendered every page, the browser displayed it, and the modem literally screamed in between. Then came AJAX, which promised dynamic pages and delivered a spaghettified mix of server and client logic. Meanwhile, the large server-side frameworks like JavaServer Faces and ASP.NET MVC did their best to tame the AJAX beast.

Some 5 years later came the real XHR revolution with single-page apps (Backbone, Angular, Ember, React). This moved the goalposts by shifting everything to the browser, along with a loading spinner from (JS) hell to keep you company while megabytes of JavaScript, markup and stylesheets trickled through the wire.

Today, frameworks like SvelteKit, React and Next.js are proudly - and with bravado - rendering pages on the server again. As if they just invented it.

Have we come full circle, or have we actually learned something useful along the way? 

In this lightning talk we take a fast, lighthearted and slightly nostalgic trip through three decades of web development. It starts with Perl scripts and 28.8k modems and ends with hydration and server components. Along the way we will look at which ideas keep coming back and, more importantly: why. 

Bring your own nostalgia for dial-up tones.
