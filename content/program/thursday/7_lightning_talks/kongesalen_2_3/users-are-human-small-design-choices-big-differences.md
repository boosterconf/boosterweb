We build features and applications and expect people to use them the way we intended. Often they don't. They skip steps, miss what we thought was obvious, or give up along the way. That's rarely because users are careless. It's because people are human: we rely on habits, follow the path of least resistance, and make most decisions without thinking too hard about them.

In this lightning talk, I'll draw on behavioural economics and UX to look at how design shapes what people do, whether we intend it to or not. We'll touch on why familiar feels safe, how small obstacles can make a big difference, and how nudging can guide users in a good direction without tricking them.

Many of these choices are made early in the process, often by developers, without anyone noticing they're design decisions at all. That's why UX deserves a place in the project from the start.
