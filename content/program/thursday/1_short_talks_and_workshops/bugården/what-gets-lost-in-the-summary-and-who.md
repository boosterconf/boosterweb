An experiment in AI and collective sensemaking

What happens when AI tells us what happened at a conference we actually attended?

AI is increasingly used to summarise meetings, research, conversations and events. It can process more material than any of us could hold in our heads and quickly turn it into themes, patterns and key takeaways.

But synthesis is never neutral. Contradictions can become patterns. Outliers can disappear. Different interpretations can turn into apparent agreement. And a coherent summary can easily feel more authoritative than the messy material it was created from.

In this participatory lab, we will use material from Booster itself as the basis for an experiment. We will ask AI to synthesise the same material in different ways and compare what happens.

What becomes visible? What disappears? Whose perspectives survive the summary? Where does disagreement turn into consensus? And what changes when we ask AI to preserve tensions and outliers rather than optimise for coherence?

The aim is not to decide whether AI is good or bad at summarising. It is to become more deliberate about how we use AI when making sense of complex material together, and about what we might want to keep visible rather than optimise away.

The conference becomes both the material and the setting for the experiment: we can compare AI’s interpretations with a situation we have actually been part of.
