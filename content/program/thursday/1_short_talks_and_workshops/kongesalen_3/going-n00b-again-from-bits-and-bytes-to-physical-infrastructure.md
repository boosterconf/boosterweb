My entire career has been spent on software that is literally just bits and bytes. Edtech, insurtech and fintech, for lack of better descriptions. Building internet banking, among other things. Then, in August 2026, I finally met the real physical world. With a bang.

I joined Trafsys, where one of the things we make is a system that monitors and controls tunnels, bridges and other equipment on the road. It’s used 24/7 by road operators at the Road Traffic Centre (Vegtrafikksentralen).

Banking is critical infrastructure too. But going from critical digital systems to critical physical infrastructure turned out to be a much bigger change than I expected. Suddenly I had to learn how to plan releases all over again. How do you decide what to build when nobody really wants anything to change? What is it like when your customer is the public sector, and your work is part of road projects that go on for years? And since you can’t just test in production, fallback solutions and simulators suddenly matter a lot.

I’ll take you through that journey: what’s different, why it’s different, and what I’ve learned along the way.
