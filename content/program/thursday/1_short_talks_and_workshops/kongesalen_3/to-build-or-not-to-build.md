In the day and age when anyone can have AI build them a system, the work of developers shifts from "Can I build this?" to "Should I build this?"

The easier it becomes to build software, the more important it becomes to understand the consequences of putting that software into the world. Is it accessible? Is it secure? Does it solve a real problem? Who will maintain it? And does it actually fit into the organization it's a part of?

The shift from "can I?" to "should I?" changes which skills developers need. We need to understand the users, the product, and where the product is headed. Not just how to code. When developers and designers work together throughout the design and build process, we can find smaller improvements and deploy them gradually, delivering value to users along the way.

From the perspective of an internal team at NRK, we will explore what it takes to build useful software in a large organization: how different teams work together to get content out to the public, how we figure out what users actually need (not only what they ask for), how we make more with less, and how we do our best to hide the underlying complexity of the systems from the user.
